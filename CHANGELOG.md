# Changelog

The version is in `package.json` (semantic versioning: MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH) and is shown at the
foot of the page with the commit it was built from. Each user-visible change bumps it and
gets an entry here: PATCH for a fix, MINOR for something new.

## 1.18.1 — 2026-11-10

- English under the Latin: the antiphons of the coming weeks the OCO gives where the
  Liturgia Horarum has others (Advent's little hours, the third Sunday of Advent, Christ the
  King, the eve of 8 December) and Eastertide's Alleluia have their translation; so do the
  hymns Iesu redemptor omnium, Iesu corona celsior and Verbum salutis omnium. The Advent
  hymns from 17 December are paired with iBreviary's English.

## 1.18.0 — 2026-11-10

- Lauds on Fridays has its psalms again: iBreviary closes the heading of the psalmody after
  "Ant. 1" there, and the whole psalmody was lost (the three antiphons stood alone). The
  little hours have their first psalm, which a like slip of the page hid.
- The little hours in Advent, Lent and Christmastide, on feasts and solemnities: the psalms
  are there (they were missing wherever the hour has one antiphon), sung under the season's
  antiphon from the OCO ("Prophetæ prædicaverunt", "Advenerunt nobis"…), which was left as
  text. Terce and Sext end with the collect on Sundays, feasts and in the seasons, where
  iBreviary prints it once, under None.
- First Vespers of the Immaculate Conception and of the Mother of God: iBreviary has none
  (7 and 31 December give the weekday's psalms and nothing more), and the hour stopped after
  the psalmody. They are made of the feast's Second Vespers with the psalms of First Vespers.
  An hour iBreviary gives cut short is taken from the psalter; a feast it leaves empty (1
  January 2027, for now) from the same feast in another year.
- The Office of Readings of feasts (St Andrew) and of 2 November has its psalms: the page has
  no heading over them.
- Beyond the window of daily texts, five psalter weekdays showed another day's office: Tuesday
  I a memorial of martyrs, Friday I St Ignatius, Thursday II St Barnabas with the First
  Vespers of the Sacred Heart, Saturday II St Anthony, Tuesday IV a Lauds cut short and the
  First Vespers of St John the Baptist. They are plain weekdays now. Where a day's own texts
  are not yet there and the psalter's stand in for them (seasons, feasts, Sundays), a line
  under the title says so.
- The Ascension and Corpus Christi on their Thursdays (General Roman Calendar) have their
  own psalms, reading, intercessions and prayer, and First Vespers on the Wednesday:
  iBreviary keeps both on the Sunday, and the Thursday had a weekday's.
- In the Triduum and the octave of Easter the antiphon in place of the responsory (Christus
  factus est, Hæc dies) is sung at Lauds and Vespers too, from the OCO's notation: a psalter
  responsory stood there. Compline within the octaves and on solemnities takes the antiphons
  of the Sunday form its psalms belong to.
- Eastertide weekdays: where the OCO has "Psalmi cum Alleluia" (Office of Readings, Lauds 1
  and 3, Vespers 1 and 2, the little hours, Compline) the antiphon is Alleluia in the psalm's
  own tone; an antiphon that merely begins with the word was sung there.
- Hymns with their score: Pange lingua … prœlium certaminis (Office of Readings in Holy
  Week), Christe cælorum Domine, Sacris sollemniis, Legis sacratæ, O lux salutis nuntia.
- Good Friday and Holy Saturday sing their own antiphons and hymns: they were resolved as the
  Friday and Saturday of Easter week. The Saturday of the Easter octave has the First Vespers
  of the octave Sunday, which keeps the octave's antiphons.
- Sundays whose Gospel antiphons the OCO's index gives by the year alone (Advent III and IV,
  the little hours of the Sundays of Lent) or abbreviates ("Amen a. d. vobis…": 37 antiphons,
  among them the 18th Sunday of year B) now find them. On a Sunday of Advent from 17 December
  the Benedictus antiphon is the Sunday's and the Magnificat's the O antiphon; at First
  Vespers the O antiphon is the evening's own (it was the morrow's).
- Where the day's own texts are not at hand and the OCO gives no Gospel antiphon, the psalter
  day's antiphon is no longer printed in its place on Sundays and in the seasons.
- Office of Readings: only the words a reading quotes are in italics (a paragraph with a
  quotation was all in italics). Its responsories find their GregoBase score whatever the
  books' spelling and with one word differing ("Devastavit" / "Exterminavit vineam"), from
  more books: 22 of the window's 84, from 18.
- Scores: a hyphen wherever the syllables of a word stand apart, and none of the gaps that
  split words after ℟ and ℣, in "Dómino" of the Benedicamus, at "sǽcula" ("Dó mine", "A men").
- At the foot of each hour, links to the hour before and after; the browser's Back and Forward
  move between the hours visited; another hour or day opens at its beginning.
- Settings in groups (Language, Office, Chant, Display, Print and help); "Calendar" for the
  Ordo. Scores the Antiphonale Romanum II has are labelled with it before the Monasticum.

## 1.17.0 — 2026-11-07

- Setting "Ordo": the General Roman Calendar or the Diocese of Hong Kong's 🇭🇰 (the Epiphany,
  the Ascension and Corpus Christi on Sunday; St Francis Xavier and St Thérèse as feasts; Our
  Lady of China, Our Lady Help of Christians, the Holy Martyrs and Blesseds of China, Sts
  Joseph Freinademetz and Gabriele Allegra, the dedication of the cathedral). A first visit
  from Hong Kong opens in Chinese with the diocese's calendar.
- Intercessions: sung in full notation, in abbreviated notation as the Antiphonale Romanum II
  writes its tones (the start and cadences notated, hollow notes for the recitation), or text.
- PDF: the number of pages (and of sheets for a booklet) shows before the PDF is made, and
  follows the format and print size; while it is made the pages count up in order.

## 1.16.0 — 2026-11-07

- Translation: five choices: Latin only, Chinese (Hong Kong), English, Chinese and English,
  or Chinese with English only where Hong Kong's office has nothing.
- Memorials: whichever the setting takes for the short reading, responsory and intercessions
  (the weekday's or the common's), its translation is shown, from the translated psalter for
  the weekday.
- Interface in English, Chinese (Hong Kong) or Latin: the hours, parts, ranks and messages,
  the settings and the help; in Chinese the celebrations are named as the Hong Kong
  diocese names them, with the Latin under the name.

## 1.15.0 — 2026-11-07

- Translation: Chinese (Hong Kong), from the Hong Kong Diocesan Liturgy Commission's daily
  office, set in Noto Serif TC, in the PDF too. Only Hong Kong's own text is shown: no
  Chinese where the office sung differs from Hong Kong's, and no hymn that is not a
  translation of the Latin one.
- Hymn translations: eleven more recognised as translations (Conditor alme siderum, Deus
  creator omnium, Verbum supernum…); two hymns opening alike are no longer confused.
- On memorials that take the weekday's reading and intercessions, their translation is
  shown only when the translated office takes the same.

## 1.14.0 — 2026-11-07

- The canticle of Ap 19 at Sunday II Vespers outside Ordinary Time (Advent, Eastertide,
  feasts) is sung with its Alleluias too, as the Antiphonale Romanum II prints it (pp. 10,
  40, 238), between the day's antiphon.
- Setting "Pange lingua": the Liber Hymnarius melody or the old Vatican edition's, which the
  AR II gives as an alternative (appendix p. 773).

## 1.13.0 — 2026-11-07

- Intercessions sung on the Antiphonale Romanum II's tone (p. 758), the introduction and
  each intention followed by the response; setting "Intercessions" (sung or text).

## 1.12.0 — 2026-11-07

- Benedictus and Magnificat: the ornate mediant on every verse, as the Antiphonale Romanum II
  sets them out (it was on the first verse only); tones III, IV and V corrected to the book.
- Salus et gloria (Sunday II Vespers per annum): week IV takes the AR II's own melody, week
  II's (it took week III's).

## 1.11.0 — 2026-11-07

- Short reading and collect: sung on the Antiphonale Romanum II's tones by default, or on the
  Liber Usualis', or as text (settings "Short reading", "Collect"). The collect's conclusion
  follows the AR II's own cadences ("Spíritus Sánc-ti" si-la, the end falling to la).
- The Antiphonale Romanum II wherever it gives a piece, the Antiphonale Monasticum only where
  it is silent: Deus in adiutorium, Pater noster, Dominus vobiscum, Dominus nos benedicat (now
  the book's own), and Benedicamus Domino by the day (Our Lady's feasts, the Sundays of
  Advent and Lent, of Eastertide, per annum); Ite in pace, alleluia, alleluia in the octave of
  Easter and on Pentecost.

## 1.10.0 — 2026-11-07

- Setting "Office of Readings hymn": by the hour (the night's before 7 in the morning), by
  day, or at night or early in the morning. The other hymn is no longer named in a rubric,
  nor are alternative hymns ("Or: …") elsewhere.

## 1.9.0 — 2026-11-07

- Versicles on Sundays, solemnities, feasts and memorials (before the readings of the Office
  of Readings, at the little hours) in the Liber Usualis' ornate first tone, with its neume on
  the last syllable; other days keep the simple tone.

## 1.8.0 — 2026-11-07

- Sung readings and collects follow the Liber Usualis throughout, as it assigns its tones in
  the Office: the short reading on the tone for the Chapter (at Compline the tone for the
  short lesson); the collect in the festal tone at the Office of Readings, Lauds and Vespers
  of Sundays, solemnities, feasts and memorials, in the ancient simple tone at the little
  hours, Compline and on ferias, in the second ferial tone after the antiphons of Our Lady.

## 1.7.0 — 2026-11-07

- Office of Readings: the responsories GregoBase has, above all from the Nocturnale
  Romanum (Impetum inimicorum today, Lapides pretiosi for the Lateran…): over a thousand
  are known now, about one a day in the coming weeks.
- After each antiphon a custos shows the first note of its psalm or canticle.
- Responsory scores draw their asterisk and cross.
- Mutual enrichment: no "ex more, ad libitum" notes in the hours; a pixel spark, and a
  burst of pixels when an option is ticked.

## 1.6.0 — 2026-11-07

- Hymns with a score where they had only text: Sunday's Office of Readings in weeks II and
  IV (Salve dies), the common of pastors (Christe pastorum caput, Inclitus rector, Vir celse,
  Dum sacerdotum, Hi sacerdotes, Sacrata nobis, in the form for a pope, a bishop or a
  priest), and the other hymns the OCO names only in its index (51 in all).
- Compline in Ordinary Time: Te lucis or Christe qui splendor by the psalter week, in the
  melody of the day's rank (Sunday, solemnity, feast, memorial, feria), Saturday evening
  being the Sunday's; fully notated with the Liber Hymnarius text.
- Mutual enrichment: ℟. Deo gratias after the short reading.
- iBreviary's rubrics on the invitatory ("Quando sequens psalmus adhibitus est ad
  Invitatorium…") are left out: the app places the invitatory itself, always with Ps 94.

## 1.5.0 — 2026-11-07

- Settings "Short reading" and "Collect": text, or sung. The short reading on the tone of
  the Antiphonale Romanum II (tonus lectionis) or on the Liber Usualis' tone for the

## 1.4.1 — 2026-11-07

- Compline in the Triduum and the octave of Easter: the antiphon the OCO puts in place of
  the responsory (Christus factus est, longer each day; then Hæc dies), not In manus tuas.
- "obœ́diens", "prœ́lia" and the like no longer print as "obǼdiens" in scores read from the OCO.

## 1.4.0 — 2026-11-07

- "Mutual enrichment" in the settings: bonus content from the older Roman Office, each
  marked *ex more, ad libitum* in the hour: Fidelium animæ at the end of the hours;
  after the antiphon of Our Lady at Compline its versicle, collect and Divinum auxilium;
  the antiphon of Our Lady by the traditional calendar (Alma to 1 February, Ave Regina
  to Wednesday of Holy Week, none in the Triduum, Regina cæli to the Friday after
  Pentecost).
- Without that option the antiphon of Our Lady follows the present seasons: Salve Regina
  in all of Ordinary Time (it was Alma in January and Ave Regina in February).

## 1.3.1 — 2026-11-07

- Invitatory: the antiphon is printed once, with the rubric "Antiphona repetitur", not twice.

## 1.3.0 — 2026-11-07

- Invitatory: the antiphon is sung twice before the psalm, as the General Instruction
  (no. 34) has it. When it ends with "Veníte, adorémus", only those words are sung after
  each strophe and the whole antiphon after the Gloria Patri (Liber Hymnarius, 2019).
- Invitatory in modes IV, IV* and IV**: the psalm is now cut into its strophes with the
  antiphon between them; it ran on as one piece.

## 1.2.1 — 2026-11-07

- Salus et gloria in psalter weeks I and IV: the Antiphonale Romanum II melody of p. 480,
  as the Klein Graduale's Sunday booklets sing it, instead of week II's.
- "O lux beata Trinitas" (Sunday Vespers, weeks II and IV) is sung from the Antiphonale
  Romanum II (p. 466), the Liber Hymnarius's score not being on GregoBase.

## 1.2.0 — 2026-11-07

- Sunday Vespers in Ordinary Time: the canticle Salus et gloria (Ap 19) is sung with its
  Alleluias as one score from the Antiphonale Romanum II, as the OCO prescribes, instead of
  the text with an antiphon. Weeks II and III have their own setting; weeks I and IV,
  not yet on GregoBase, sing week II's.
- Scores no longer print "℟.." and "℣.." with a doubled full stop.

## 1.1.0 — 2026-11-06

- Sound: the ▶ now plays with the iPhone's ringer switch on silent (the page plays as
  media: `navigator.audioSession` on iOS 17+, unmute.js before that), and wakes the audio
  after a call or a locked screen.
- Version number at the foot of the page.
- Visitor counts (number of visitors, their countries) with GoatCounter: open source,
  no cookies, no personal data.
- English translation (beta) in the settings, laid out as Breviarium Gregorianum: psalms
  verse by verse, antiphons, hymns, readings, responsories, intercessions and collect
  under the Latin. iBreviary's English where the OCO sings the Liturgia Horarum's text;
  the app's own translation in the same manner where the OCO's text differs (279
  antiphons and responsories of the coming weeks) and for hymns iBreviary has no English for.
- Christ the King: its hymn at II Vespers as at I Vespers (the index gave it to I Vespers
  only).
- Hymns without a Liber Hymnarius score on GregoBase no longer borrow another hymn's score
  that begins alike ("O lux beata Trinitas" had the score of "O lux beata Caelitum",
  "Pange lingua … certaminis" that of the Corpus Christi hymn); their text is shown.

## 1.0.0 — 2026-11-05

Every hour of every day of the General Roman Calendar in chant after the Ordo Cantus
Officii 2015: psalms pointed to their tones, propers of seasons, feasts and saints, daily
iBreviary texts; ▶ on every score; PDF pages and booklets with a print size; the source
of every score and a help page; icons for every platform.
